1. The look

Sea of Thieves meets Fortnite meets Kena: real materials and real light, chunky readable shapes, something moving in every frame. The hen and her eggs are always the brightest thing on screen.

Target frames are AI concept art, not in-game captures. The camera in the dusk frame sits lower than the game camera (pitch 52°).

What makes it read as real (in priority order)

  1. Light does the work.Warm sun, cool shadows, real sky light on materials (PBR back on). Contrast comes from light, not from saturated paint.
  2. Materials, not colours.Bark has grooves, stone has cracks, paths have pebbles and ruts. A flat colour anywhere big reads as cheap.
  3. Density at the edges, calm in the middle.Grass, flowers, rocks and roots crowd the borders and props. The play area stays open so the hen and eggs pop.
  4. Something moves everywhere.Grass blades, leaves, pollen, water, clouds, lantern flicker. Still frames look like a screenshot of a demo.
  5. Sound sells the space.Wind that rises with the gusts, the shore getting louder at the coast, footsteps that change with the ground. Section 6.
Not possible in Roblox here: the soft background blur (depth of field) in the play frame. Roblox caps focus distance at 200 studs and our near-orthographic camera sits about 620 studs away, so it would blur the whole screen. Depth comes from Atmosphere haze, and foreground framing comes from real props near the screen edge.

Why these numbers

With sky light on, the sky lights every surface, so the flat ambient lift drops and shadows get real depth. Specular at 0.65 gives wet stone and glossy eggs a sheen without plastic shine. Saturation comes down from 0.36 to 0.14 because real materials already carry colour. Haze 1.4 gives distance depth now that depth of field is out.

4. Ground and materials

Terrain (walkable tops)

  • Override terrain materials with the project's Painted* variants (PaintedGrass, PaintedGround, PaintedRock, PaintedSand…), not the Flat* ones.
  • Terrain.Decoration = true and GrassLength = 0.75. This is Roblox's own swaying grass, cheap on the GPU, set by the preset.
  • Two grass tones per biome: a lit tone and a cooler shade tone in noise patches. Never one flat green field.
  • Paths: Ground or Mud material with pebble decals, and ruts painted into the variant's ColorMap.

Meshes

  • SurfaceAppearance on every hero and prop: ColorMap + NormalMap + RoughnessMap. Metalness 0 except iron lamps.
  • Texture budget: 1024 px landmarks, 512 px props, 256 px small dressing. Trim sheets shared across props.
  • Rocks get a moss top (vertex colour or a second material) and sit 0.3 studs into the ground. Nothing floats.
  • Subtle studs only on man-made props (owner decision C).

6. Sound

Code: src/client/Soundscape.luau, ids in src/client/AmbienceIds.luau (picked by roblox/tools/audio/find_sounds.py from free, licensed Creator Store libraries only). The mix follows the hen, the hour and the weather, so it is right everywhere without per-place scripting.

How it works

  • Listener on the hen. The camera is about 620 studs away, so 3D sounds are heard from the hen's position, oriented to the camera.
  • Beds: looping layers crossfaded with a 1.2 s ease by zone × context (coast distance, trees nearby, gusts, rain, night).
  • Spots: 3D loops on real things: the nearest pond, and the two nearest lanterns at night.
  • One-shots: random, in 3D around the hen, never the same take twice in a row, ±8 % pitch.
  • Footsteps: every hop landing of the local hen; the surface comes from the tile under her.

Mix rules

  • Volume tiers: UI 0.8 · rewards 1.0 · ambience 0.8 · music 0.6 (Sfx categories; the Settings slider uses Sfx.setVolume("ambience", v)).
  • Behind any open window: Soundscape.setMuffled(true) low-passes the world (high −22 dB, mid −6 dB).
  • Big moments (hatch, island unlock): Soundscape.duck(0.4, 2).
  • Reverb per zone: none in the open, Forest, Quarry (Mine), Mountains (Expedition), Auditorium (Echo), Cave.
  • At most about 12 sounds playing at once. Beds stop when silent.

8. First Studio session (owner home)

  1. Sync (tools/manifest.json includes WorldTree, Soundscape, LightingPresets, AmbienceIds), press Play and check Output is clean.
  2. Run the hierarchy audit in the command bar and fix what it lists:
    print(table.concat(require(game.ReplicatedStorage.Peckwood.WorldTree).audit(), "\n"))
  3. Look: compare golden and toy (PeckwoodLook) at 07:00, 12:00, 17:30 and 21:00 (PeckwoodHour), with screenshots next to the targets.
  4. Grass: blades visible and swaying with the gusts. If too tall or dense for the near-ortho camera, lower GrassLength to 0.5.
  5. Listen pass (headphones): Park middle, Park coast, under a tree, by the pond, at night by a lantern, in the rain (PeckwoodWeather), on a deck and in the Mine. Note each slot to swap.
  6. Footsteps: one soft tap per hop, never a double hit. If a take double-hits, trim with the 0.18 s cap or pick an alternate.
  7. Performance: MicroProfiler on the phone emulator with every island open, within the budget table.
