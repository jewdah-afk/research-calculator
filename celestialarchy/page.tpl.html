What the sim found

  1. Restart at a bar start can lock players in a death loop. The spec restarts at the beginning of the current 4-section bar, but the bar's first obstacle can sit only 24 studs in. At the restart speed of 42 studs/s that leaves about 0.57 s, which allows roughly 10.7 studs of lateral travel. The route certificate assumed up to 20. With a spawn at the shaft center, 99 of 900 fuzz runs never finished and racked up 51,258 deaths. The fix in the sim spawns the player lined up with the bar's first certified gap: 900 of 900 finish and deaths drop to 744. The spec needs a rule for this, either aligned spawn or a full 1.2 s telegraph ahead of every checkpoint.
  2. About 6 obstacles per run fall back to the open archetype. All 8 candidates fail certification when the next section's gap is far from the previous one. Fallback works as designed (0 bad certificates), but it makes about 6% of obstacles trivial. Consider sampling gap centers near the previous gap instead of uniformly.
  3. The sensory cap held on every 20 Hz frame, including slider 0, where all added modulation reduces to zero and only obstacle motion stays.
  4. Determinism holds. The same seed produced identical traces on both runs. H32 uses 16-bit limb multiplies only.
  5. Every arithmetic total in the spec checks out (budget sums, triangle counts, packet sizes, raw section bytes, the I* table).

Not simulated yet: mesh building, wall breathing and choreography poses, impossible geometry, netcode. These are 2D gate-and-gap rules on a scripted test pilot that makes a deliberate mistake on about 5% of obstacles.
