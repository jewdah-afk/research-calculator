What the render shows

  1. Only the next obstacle is a threat (T1). Every later obstacle drops to wall structure (T4), then to distant ambient (T6) past 256 studs. Exactly one red object is on screen at a time.
  2. Saturated red cannot reach T1 brightness. The brightest pure red has luminance 0.21; T1 edges need 0.68 to 0.80. The projection lowers saturation to 0.15 to hit the band, so the T1 edge reads pale rose. The red is carried by the darker interior (saturation 0.90) and the angular chevrons. The spec should say whether T1 means edge luminance or hue, because it cannot have both at full saturation.
  3. Breathing walls move on the floor and ceiling only, in 2-stud steps at 0.25 Hz. The player travels laterally at mid-height, so this is the corridor exclusion: side walls stay put.
  4. Speed streaks (T5) never enter the protected region: the central 40% of the width, plus the T0 to T2 bounds expanded by 12 px. The audit counts any rejected streaks per frame.
  5. Obstacles fade as you reach them. An obstacle at point-blank range fills about 60% of the screen. A saturated-red plate sweeping past would be the large red-area change that WCAG's red-flash rule is about. The face fades over its last 40 studs and the outline stays. The spec doesn't cover this yet.
  6. Rewards (T3) sit 16 studs past their obstacle on the gap line, so they never overlap a T1 shape.
