# The Glasshouse

**SHOT 3:** The campers glance at the note, then at each other. One of them looks away.

**SHOT 4:** The camera slowly pushes in on **ROWAN**, sitting apart from the others and sharpening a blade. They stop. They lean forward and pick up the note.

**SHOT 5:** Close-up on Rowan's face as they read it twice.

## The Empty Tent

The tent is gone.

The crates are gone.

The small table where the seeds had been laid out is gone.

All that remains is a patch of flattened grass.

And, in the middle of it, a single seed.

Beneath it is a folded piece of paper.

The handwriting is neat.

It isn't the old lady's.

**"She'd want you to have this."**

There is no name.

The player looks at Rowan.

Rowan looks away.

---

## Thornveil

The trail beyond the orchard is barely a trail at all.

Brambles have grown over it from both sides, forming tall walls of thorns.

The campers have a name for this part of the forest.

**Thornveil.**

Rowan leads the way.

They move carefully, cutting through the thickest vines and pointing out the ones the player should avoid.

Some vines are ordinary.

Others twitch when touched.

A few curl slowly toward the player's hands.

"Don't stand still," Rowan says.

The player soon learns why.

When they look back, the path they had just cut is already closing behind them.

---

Things live in the thorns.

Small creatures, covered in spines, scuttle between the brambles.

Clusters of spore pods hang from the branches, waiting to burst.

Once, the ground beneath the player's feet shifts. Rowan grabs their arm and pulls them aside just as a thick root breaks through the soil where they had been standing.

Rowan doesn't seem surprised.

"It's worse than last time," they say.

"It's closer."

---

## The Glasshouse

Then, through the thorns, light.

Not sunlight.

Reflected light.

Glass.

The brambles open into a wide clearing, and in the middle of it stands a building the player never expected to find this deep in the forest.

A greenhouse.

An enormous one.

Its iron frame curves high overhead, and hundreds of glass panels catch the fading light.

At least, the ones that remain.

Many of the panels are shattered.

Not from the outside.

From the inside.

Vines and branches spill out through the broken glass as though the building could no longer hold whatever was growing within it.

Rowan stops at the edge of the clearing.

"This is where I turned back," they say.

The player steps forward.

After a moment, Rowan follows.

---

The inside of the greenhouse is warm and humid.

Water drips from the ceiling.

Rows of long wooden tables stretch across the floor, covered in seed trays, clay pots, and glass jars.

Most of the plants have grown far beyond their containers.

Some have grown into the tables themselves.

Charts are pinned to the walls.

Hand-drawn diagrams of leaves.

Of roots.

Of mutations.

Each one is labelled in the same careful handwriting.

The old lady.

The cabin had been where she lived.

But this is where she worked.

---

At the back of the greenhouse is a door.

It is made of heavy metal, completely out of place among the wood and glass.

It is sealed.

Beside it is a small panel with an empty slot.

The player recognizes the shape.

They take out the metal container from the orchard.

The preserved sample.

It fits perfectly.

The panel hums.

Somewhere inside the wall, something old begins to turn.

Then the door opens.

---

The room beyond is small and clean.

Cleaner than anything else in the forest.

In the center sits a large glass chamber surrounded by tubes and dials.

An incubator.

Beside it are shelves of labelled seeds, carefully sorted and stored.

Some are ordinary.

Most are not.

Everything the old lady had learned about the forest.

Everything she had grown.

It had all been kept here.

---

Above the desk in the corner of the room, a photograph is pinned to the wall.

It is faded and curled at the edges.

It shows two people standing in front of the cabin.

One of them is the old lady, many years younger.

She is smiling.

Beside her is someone else, younger still, wearing field gear and holding a tray of seedlings.

Through the cabin window behind them, the kitchen table is just visible.

Two chairs.

Both pulled out.

The player looks more closely at the second person.

They know that face.

It's the camper who sold them the seed.

"I didn't know for sure," Rowan says quietly.

"But I wondered."

The player takes the photograph down and puts it away.

---

On the desk is a final stack of notes.

The pages are ordered.

Careful.

Calm.

These were not written in a hurry.

The old lady writes that she was wrong about the Heart.

She had spent years searching for the place where everything was growing toward.

A clearing.

A tree.

A source.

But there was no single place.

The roots from Mossy Hollow.

The roots from the orchard.

The roots beneath the Glasshouse.

They were all connected.

One enormous network, stretching beneath the entire forest.

Every mutated plant was part of it.

Every seed that sprouted in the forest's soil became part of it.

Near the bottom of the page, one line has been underlined twice.

**"The garden at the cabin too."**

The player stares at the page.

Every seed they had planted.

Every plant they had harvested.

Every mutation they had grown.

It had all been growing into the same roots.

The Heart wasn't somewhere in the forest.

The Heart *was* the forest.

And the player had been feeding it.

---

Beneath the final page is one more.

It is short.

**"I'm going down to meet it."**

**"If I don't come back, don't blame Theo."**

**"I asked him to keep the seeds moving."**

**"It needs to be fed slowly. Carefully. Or it will wake all at once."**

The player reads it again.

Theo.

The camper.

He hadn't been selling mutated seeds to trick anyone.

He had been doing exactly what she asked.

Rowan kneels beside the desk and brushes away a layer of dirt and leaves from the floor.

There is a hatch.

Old wood, bound with iron.

Thick roots have grown around its edges.

But it has clearly been opened before.

---

## The Escape

Behind them, the incubator hums.

Louder.

Then louder still.

Something inside it has started to grow.

The floor trembles.

Out in the main greenhouse, the glass begins to rattle.

Roots shift beneath the floorboards.

Not attacking.

Not yet.

Waking.

"Fed slowly," Rowan says, staring at the incubator.

"Or it wakes all at once."

They both understand at the same time.

Opening the door had not been slow.

"Go," Rowan says.

"Now."

---

The run back through Thornveil is worse than the walk in.

The vines no longer wait for the player to stand still.

They reach.

The paths they cut on the way in have already closed.

Rowan hacks a way through while the player keeps the creatures back.

Behind them, a panel of the Glasshouse shatters.

Then another.

By the time they reach the orchard, the sun has set.

The trees are silent.

Too silent.

Every branch is leaning the same way.

Not toward the Glasshouse.

Away from it.

Toward the cabin.

---

## The Cabin Holds

The cabin looks the same as when the player left it.

The repaired wall.

The cleaned-up crafting bench.

The organized shed.

The garden, green and healthy.

Rowan stops at the door and puts a hand on the wall.

They knock on it.

It's solid.

Much thicker than a cabin wall needs to be.

Rowan walks around the side of the house.

There are old marks on the outer wall.

Deep scratches.

Scrapes.

The kind of marks roots leave when they push against something for a long time.

Rowan looks at the shed.

At the stacks of boards the player had sorted.

The fence posts.

The tools.

"She didn't build a cabin," Rowan says slowly.

"She built a wall."

"Repair what you can," the player remembers.

It had never been about making the place comfortable.

---

The garden moves.

At first, just slightly.

The leaves of the player's own plants turn, all at once, toward the forest.

Then the soil at the edge of the property splits open.

Roots.

Dozens of them.

Behind them, creatures of bark and thorn crawl out of the dark.

They aren't coming for the player.

They're coming for the garden.

For the cabin.

For everything the player has grown here.

The forest wants it back.

Rowan draws their blade and takes a position by the garden gate.

"Everything you fixed," they say, "we're about to find out if it was enough."

The player is forced to defend the cabin.

---

The night is long.

The roots hit the walls, and the walls hold.

They tear at the fences, and the fences slow them.

The old tools from the shed become traps.

Barricades go up in gaps the player never thought would matter.

The creatures come in waves.

And each time, a little less of the forest makes it past the garden.

---

When the sun finally rises, the attack stops.

Not suddenly.

Slowly.

The roots pull back into the soil one by one.

The creatures retreat into the trees.

The player watches them go.

They are all moving in the same direction.

Not deeper into the forest.

Back toward the Glasshouse.

Back toward the hatch.

---

Inside, the cabin is quiet again.

The player takes out the photograph and pins it to the wall above the kitchen table.

Two people.

Two chairs.

Rowan looks at it for a long moment.

Then, for the first time since the player arrived, someone pulls out the second chair and sits down.

"She went down there to stop this," Rowan says.

"Or to understand it."

"Either way, she didn't come back up."

The player looks out the window.

Past the garden.

Past the orchard.

Toward the place where the roots had gone.

Far below the forest, something moves.

Slowly.

Like breathing.

The old lady hadn't disappeared.

She had gone down.

And the path she took is still open.
